Have you ever...?

Think of something amazing you did!

Show the picture. Elicit: 'Have you ever eaten something strange?' (2 min). ICQ: Are you thinking or writing?

Present Perfect — Form

have / has + past participle

Write on board: I have visited. She has eaten. They have seen. Drill pronunciation of participles. (3 min)

Ever and Never

Ever = in your life? Never = not in my life

Example: 'Have you ever been to the beach?' 'I have never been to the beach.' Elicit more examples. (3 min)

Affirmative

I have seen that movie.

Show affirmative. Point out: have + past participle 'seen'. (1 min)

Negative

I have not seen that movie.

Point out: NOT goes between have and past participle. (1 min)

Question

Have I seen that movie?

Point out: HAVE moves to the start. (1 min)

Choose the correct form

She ___ (has / have) visited London.

Show fragment first (blank), next click reveals answer 'has'. Wait 5 seconds between clicks. (2 min). ICQ: Are you writing or just looking?

Unscramble the sentence

Students write the correct order. Then the words fly into place. (2 min)

Ask your partner!

Have you ever eaten Thai food? Have you ever gone to the beach? Have you ever seen a ghost?

Pair work. Student A asks, Student B answers 'Yes, I have / No, I haven't.' (4 min). Monitor and take notes for error correction.

Report your partner

She has eaten Thai food. He has never gone to the beach.

Students report to class: 'She has eaten Thai food.' 'He has never gone to the beach.' (3 min). Expected: have/has + past participle.

Two Truths, One Lie

Write three things you have done. One is not true. Your partner guesses the lie!

Example: 'I have climbed a mountain. I have eaten snake. I have flown a plane.' Partners guess the lie. (5 min)
