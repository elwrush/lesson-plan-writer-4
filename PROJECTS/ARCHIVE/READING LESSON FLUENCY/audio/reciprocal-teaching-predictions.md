### Reciprocal Teaching — Predictions Audio

*Classroom listening: the teacher introduces the reciprocal-reading prediction step, then two students (B1 and B2) make a prediction from the heading. Used with the Reciprocal Teaching worksheet.*

| Character | Voice notes | Key line |
|:----------|:-------------|:---------|
| **Benedict** | Teacher, warm and clear, authoritative classroom narrator. | "Let's listen to how students can talk to each other about reading." |
| **Emma** | B1 teenage girl, natural and curious, reading her prediction aloud. | "The heading is 'What Is Going Wrong — the causes of the decline'." |
| **Oliver** | B2 teenage boy, thoughtful, slightly more formal, uses more complex language. | "The heading tells me this chunk will explain why reading is falling, before any solutions." |

**Benedict:** Let's listen to how students can talk to each other about reading. We will start with the B1 reading.

**Benedict:** The only person to speak before reading is The Predictor. They will look at the reading headings and illustrations and say what they think the story will be about, and why they have that opinion.

**Benedict:** In the example on your worksheet, the heading is: "What Is Going Wrong — the causes of the decline". Listen to the predictions. First, Emma, in the B1 story.

**Emma:** The heading is "What Is Going Wrong — the causes of the decline". So I predict this chunk will talk about why people read less. Maybe phones?

**Benedict:** Now for Oliver in the B2 reading. Note how he uses more complex language.

**Oliver:** The heading "What Is Going Wrong — the causes of the decline" tells me this chunk will explain why reading is falling, before any solutions. I predict the causes will be technological.

**Benedict:** After this, you will all read the chunk silently. After this, the Clarifier, Questioner and Summariser will discuss what they have read.

**Benedict:** Let's listen to the B1 example first.

**Benedict:** Now for the B2 example.
