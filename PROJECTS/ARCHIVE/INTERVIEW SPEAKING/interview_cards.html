1Interview Question 1 — Personal
Tell me about yourself.
Answer structure
  1. Who you are: My name is… I am 14 years old.
  2. What you like: I enjoy… (sports, music, reading)
  3. Your goal: In the future, I want to…
Follow-up: “What do you do in your free time?” “What is your favourite subject?”
2Interview Question 2 — Why MUIDS?
Why do you want to study at MUIDS?
Answer structure
  1. Answer first: Because of the way they teach. Students learn by doing things, not just reading.
  2. Example: For example, science students do real experiments and present them in English.
  3. For me: I want to study medicine, so I need strong English and practical skills.
  4. Close: So MUIDS is exactly the school I want.
Follow-up: “What do you know about MUIDS?” “Which subjects interest you?”
3Interview Question 3 — Favourite subject
What is your favourite subject and why?
Answer structure
  1. P — Position: Science, for sure!
  2. R — Reason: I love knowing how things work.
  3. E — Example: For example, last term we did an experiment with magnets.
  4. P — Position: So yeah, that’s why science is my favourite.
Follow-up: “What do you like most about it?” “Which subject do you find difficult?”
4Interview Question 4 — Opinion
Should mobile phones be banned at school?
Answer structure
  1. P — Position: Hmm, no. I don’t think phones should be banned.
  2. R — Reason: They’re really useful for learning.
  3. E — Example: For example, I can look up a word on my phone in seconds.
  4. P — Position: So I think they should stay, just not during tests.
Follow-up: “How much do you use your phone?” “What do you use it for?” “Do you think phone use is important?”
5Interview Question 5 — Challenge
What do you do when you face a challenge?
Answer structure
  1. S — Situation: Tell about a hard time. For example, a difficult exam.
  2. A — Action: Say what you did. I made a study plan and practiced every day.
  3. R — Result: Say what happened. I passed, and I felt proud.
Follow-up: “What did you learn from it?” “Would you do anything differently?”
6Interview Question 6 — Strengths & weaknesses
What are your strengths and weaknesses?
Answer structure
  1. Strength: I am good at… (e.g. working in a team)
  2. Example: For example, I help my group in school projects.
  3. Weakness: One weakness is… I worry too much about exams.
  4. Improving: I am working on it. I practice deep breathing before tests.
Follow-up: “How are you improving your weakness?” “Who helps you when things are hard?”
7Interview Question 7 — About MUIDS
What do you know about MUIDS?
Answer structure
  1. Facts: MUIDS is an international high school at Mahidol University.
  2. Learning: Students study in English and learn by doing projects and experiments.
  3. Example: For example, science students do lab experiments and present their results in English.
  4. Why it matters: That’s why MUIDS is my first choice.
Follow-up: “Where is MUIDS?” “What subjects can you study there?”
8Interview Question 8 — Your questions
Do you have any questions for us?
Answer structure
  1. About learning: What is a normal day at MUIDS like?
  2. About activities: What clubs and sports can students join?
  3. About support: How do teachers help new students?
Follow-up: “Ask one or two questions. Asking shows you are interested.”
9Interview Question 9 — Social media
Should social media be banned for teenagers under 16?
Answer structure
  1. P — Position: Hmm, no. I don’t think a full ban is the answer.
  2. R — Reason: Social media helps us stay in touch and learn new things.
  3. E — Example: For example, I follow science pages and chat with my friends.
  4. P — Position: So I think age limits and clear rules are better than a ban.
Follow-up: “How much time do you spend on social media?” “What are the dangers of social media?” “Do your parents limit your screen time?”
10Interview Question 10 — Homework
Should homework be banned?
Answer structure
  1. P — Position: Hmm, not completely. Some homework is useful.
  2. R — Reason: It helps us practise what we learn in class.
  3. E — Example: For example, a little maths homework keeps my skills sharp.
  4. P — Position: But I think the amount should be smaller, not banned.
Follow-up: “How much homework do you get?” “What kind of homework do you like?” “When is homework a waste of time?”
