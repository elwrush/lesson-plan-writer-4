Classroom practice dialog in an interview room. Two characters: an interviewer and a 14-year-old student.
Teacher

Nice. So why do you want to study at MUIDS?
Ploy
Because of the way they teach. Students learn by doing things, not just reading. For example, science students do real experiments and present them in English. I want to study medicine, so I need strong English and practical skills. So MUIDS is exactly the school I want.
Teacher
What's your favourite subject?
Ploy
Science, for sure! I love knowing how things work. For example, last term we did an experiment with magnets. So yeah, that's why science is my favourite.
Teacher
Another question. Should phones be banned at school?
Ploy
Hmm, no. I don't think phones should be banned. They're really useful for learning. For example, I can look up a word on my phone in seconds. So I think they should stay, just not during tests.
Teacher
Good answer. What do you do when you face a challenge?
Ploy
Last year, I had a really hard maths exam. I was nervous, so I made a study plan and practiced every day. In the end, I passed, and I felt so proud.
Teacher
Very good. Do you have any questions for us?
Ploy
Yes. What's a normal day at MUIDS like?
Teacher
We start at eight thirty, and we have five classes a day. Thank you for coming. We'll let you know our decision soon.
Ploy
Thank you. Goodbye.

MUIDS Interview — B2 (Elle)

Classroom practice dialog in an interview room. Two characters: an interviewer and a 15-year-old student.
Teacher
Good morning. Please, take a seat. To start, could you tell me a little about yourself?
Elle
Good morning, thank you. My name is Elle, and I'm fifteen years old. I'm in Mathayom 3 at a school in Bangkok. I'm the vice-captain of the science club, and I play badminton for a local team. We train twice a week, which keeps me busy, but I really enjoy it. When I have time, I love reading science fiction. I'm actually halfway through a series about space exploration at the moment. Looking ahead, my dream is to study medicine abroad, so I'm trying to build strong English and science foundations now.
Teacher
Wonderful. So why do you want to study at MUIDS specifically?
Elle
To be honest, the main reason is the way they teach. At MUIDS, learning is hands-on. Students don't just memorise theories from a textbook. They design experiments, collect data, and present their findings in English. And that's exactly the kind of learning I'm looking for, because I want to study medicine, and medicine is all about applying what you know in real situations. On top of that, studying in an international environment would really push my English to the next level, which I'd need for university. So for me, MUIDS isn't just another school. It's the right path to where I want to go.
Teacher
Which subject do you enjoy the most?
Elle
Without a doubt, science, especially biology. What fascinates me is how living things work, from tiny cells to whole ecosystems. For instance, last term we grew bacteria in the lab and tested different hand-washing methods. It sounds simple, but seeing the results with our own eyes was amazing. We could actually count the colonies and see which method worked best. Although biology can be challenging, with a lot of vocabulary to remember, that's part of what makes it exciting. I never feel bored in that class.
Teacher
Interesting. Let's move on to a different topic. Do you think mobile phones should be banned at school?
Elle
That's a great question, and it's one people have very different opinions about. Personally, I don't think a complete ban is the answer, although I can understand why some schools have introduced one. Phones are genuinely useful learning tools. Students can look up information during a lesson, use translation apps when they meet a new word, or even photograph the board instead of writing everything down. However, I do agree that phones shouldn't be used everywhere. During tests, in group discussions, or when a teacher is explaining something, they should be off or put away. So, to sum up, I believe the right solution is clear rules about when phones are okay, rather than banning them completely.
Teacher
A very thoughtful answer. Could you tell me about a challenge you've faced and how you handled it?
Elle
Yes, I'd be happy to. Last year, I entered a science competition with two friends, and we discovered we only had two weeks to prepare. At first, I felt quite overwhelmed. We had to design an experiment, build a simple model, and write a report in English, and honestly, I wasn't sure we could finish in time. Instead of panicking, I sat down with my team and broke the project into small tasks, with a deadline for each one. We practised our presentation every evening after school, and we even recorded ourselves to check our English. In the end, we won second place. But the biggest lesson for me wasn't the prize. It was learning that planning beats panic, and that asking my teammates for help is a strength, not a weakness.
Teacher
Excellent. And do you have any questions for us?
Elle
Yes, I do. Two, actually. First, could you tell me what a typical day at MUIDS looks like, especially for a new Grade 10 student? And second, how does the school support students who are moving from a Thai school and might need extra help with English?
Teacher
Those are exactly the questions we like to hear. We'll make sure to cover both. Thank you for coming today. It was a pleasure.
Elle
Thank you very much. It was a pleasure to meet you too.
