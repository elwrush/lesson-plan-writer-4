# ESL Authorial Voice Rules

Apply these rules to ALL slide content. These are research-backed constraints for Thai A2-B1 learners.

## Tone

- Use conversational "you" / "we" register — not formal textbook English
- Short imperatives for instructions: "Match the words." "Listen and check."
- No passive voice — active only ("We use present perfect for experiences", not "Present perfect is used for experiences")
- No complex conditionals — avoid "If you had..." type structures

## Sentence Constraints

- **4-5 words per line** (Mayer's Segmenting Principle: break into learner-sized chunks)
- **8-12 words per sentence** for B1; 5-8 for A2
- One idea per sentence — no compound sentences with "and", "but", "because"
- No embedded clauses

## Lexical Control

- Cambridge B1 Preliminary vocabulary profile only — no words outside PET wordlist without glossing
- Define any CEFR B2+ word inline: "The **consequences** (results) were serious."
- No idioms, phrasal verbs beyond PET list, or culturally specific references
- Avoid negative polarity items ("hardly", "barely", "seldom")

## Slide Text Density

- **Max 25 words per content slide** (not counting headings)
- One concept per slide — split rather than scroll
- Use images to replace text where possible (Mayer's Multimedia Principle)

## Why

- Thai A2-B1 learners cannot process complex syntax while simultaneously parsing slide content
- Mayer's Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning: working memory is split between visual (slide) and auditory (teacher voice)
- Cambridge PET reading texts at B1 average 10-12 words per sentence

## References

- Mayer, R. E. (2021). *Multimedia Learning* (3rd ed.). Cambridge University Press.
- Cambridge University Press. (2020). *Preliminary English Test: Handbook for Teachers*.
- Nation, I. S. P. (2013). *Learning Vocabulary in Another Language*. Cambridge University Press.
