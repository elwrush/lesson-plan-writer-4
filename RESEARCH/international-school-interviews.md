# International & Scholarship School Entry Interviews (Thailand)

Research compiled 2026-08-19 via Tavily/DDGS web search. Sources listed per section.
Purpose: foundation for an interview-preparation lesson (M2/M3 classes targeting
international, IB, and scholarship schools).

## MUIDS — Mahidol University International Demonstration School (Nakhon Pathom)

US-curriculum high school (G10–12, M.4–M.6) at Mahidol University Salaya. WASC-accredited,
blends Thai MoE standards with California Common Core. Four admission rounds per year
(e.g. ACY 2027-28: Round 1 opens 10 Aug 2026; Round 4 closes 17 Mar 2027).

- Application fee: 5,000 THB (non-refundable). Online application via school portal.
- Entrance exams over **two days** (all sections mandatory; outside test scores not accepted):
  - **Saturday:** TOEFL ITP (morning, 2.5 h: Listening 50q, Structure & Written
    Expression 40q, Reading 50q) + **Essay Writing** (afternoon, 1.5 h: expository,
    300–400 words, choose 1 of 2 prompts, scored /12 on clarity, organisation, language).
  - **Sunday:** MAP test (adaptive Math + Reading, computer-based, min ~11 per school scale)
    + **Interview**.
- **Interview: 15 minutes, two teachers, scored /16.** Assesses: communication ability,
  analytical thinking, participation/engagement, personality/fit, readiness.
  Threshold guidance (Thai tutoring sources): TOEFL ITP ≥ 450/677 for consideration;
  450–469 accepted only with paid English Language Learner Program (ELL, 20,000 THB).
- Contact: muidsadmission@mahidol.ac.th, +66 (0)2-408-8555.
- Recommendation from Thai prep academies: start TOEFL + math prep ~6 months ahead.

Sources:

## MUIDS interview — detailed guidance (primary target)

**No official question list is published.** MUIDS states only that candidates sit an
"Interview" on Sunday (with the MAP test); scoring is at interviewer discretion.
The guidance below is compiled from alumni first-hand accounts and Thai tutoring
academies (Classy English Salaya, Interpass, House of Griffin, ignite by OnDemand).

**Format (as experienced by students):**
- English interview conducted by foreign teachers (mostly American), 5–15 minutes,
  typically with two teachers present.
- Two reported formats, varying by year and by room:
  - **Topic-slip format:** opens with self-introduction, then the student draws a
    label/topic and speaks about it, then follow-up questions on what they said.
    Example topic drawn: sports.
  - **Conversation format:** no slip; interviewers ask an opinion/thinking question
    and probe with follow-ups. Example: "Should mobile phones be banned at school?"
    → "How much do you use your phone? What do you use it for? Do you think phone
    use is important?"
- Scoring: full score 4 with pass at 2.8 (Interpass 2021) — other sources cite a
  different scale (16 points, The Advisor Academy 2026); the school does not publish
  the current scale, so treat "communication quality" as the criterion rather than
  a fixed score.

**Common questions reported by academies:**
1. Tell me about yourself.
2. Why do you want to study at MUIDS?
3. What is your favorite subject and why?
4. What do you do when you face a challenge?
5. Opinion questions on everyday topics (phones at school, etc.) with follow-up probes.

**What is being assessed:** speaking fluency and communication, ability to think and
extend an answer, and personality/attitude (confidence, engagement, participation).

**Advice from alumni and tutors:**
- Practice speaking naturally every day (Netflix/English media; daily-life vocabulary).
- Train for follow-up questions — interviewers dig into whatever the student says,
  so every answer should be expandable.
- Smile and make eye contact; answers need confidence, not perfection.
- Do timed mock interviews; treat "I'm not sure, but I'd like to learn more" as an
  acceptable honest answer.
- MUIDS students report the school values practical learning, inquiry, discussion,
  and open questions ("teachers ask us to discuss, not just accept") — answers that
  show curiosity and a willingness to think out loud fit the school culture.

## Triam Udom Suksa School (Bangkok)

Thailand's top state secondary school (G10–12). General admission is **exam-only** —
no interview for the standard entrance track. ~14,000 applicants for 1,520 seats (2026).

- Exam subjects (G10 entrance): typically Thai, math, science, English, social studies.
- Quota tracks for specific abilities/achievements (e.g. gifted programs) may add
  portfolio/selection steps — check each year's announcement.
- Implication for the lesson: interview skills help here mainly for quota tracks and
  as speaking prep; the written exams dominate.

## Udon Thani International School (UDIS) — local IB school

Full IB World School (PYP, MYP, DP) + National Curriculum of England, CIS-accredited,
first CIS-accredited school in NE Thailand. Only fully accredited international school
in Udon Thani. Ages 2–18.

**Enrolment process (secondary students):**
1. Registration form + 60,000 THB registration fee (refundable if not accepted, minus
   10,000 THB application fee).
2. Child attends an **observation** at the school where possible.
3. Students entering Year 1+ take **EAL and other enrolment tests** as needed.
4. **Enrolment interview** (child + parents).
5. **Year 12 DP applicants: pre-enrolment interview with the Senior School Principal.**
6. Acceptance notified after interview; term fees then due.

**Scholarships** (ages 12–17, Thai and international students; fee discounts 25/50/100%,
renewable 3 years, full discount aimed at talented students without means):
- Apply by introductory email (child info, scholarship type, fee-support level).
- Ranges: academics, language, social contribution, sports, arts, special merit.
- Selection involves interview; DP scholarships explicitly advertised ("Scholarships
  available, apply now" — Year 12 admissions).

**Thai-school transition:** late entry Term 1 (after Thai first semester, ~October) or
enrol for Term 3 (late April). Discounts apply.

## Other international schools in Thailand (general pattern)

**NIST International School (Bangkok, IB) — representative of top-tier IB schools:**
- Steps: pre-application → full application (reports, teacher recommendations, fees
  6,000 THB) → admissions committee review → **admissions assessment + interview**
  (assessment fee 2,500 THB) → acceptance.
- Secondary: grade-level test (essay + interview); Year 7+ requires intermediate-to-
  advanced English (ELA programme); beginner-level English usually not admitted at
  secondary level.
- Committee weighs: English proficiency, school records, recommendations, nationality,
  academic ability, **IB experience, world-language choice, extracurricular/community
  involvement**, and international-mindedness / "fit".

**Shrewsbury Bangkok, ISB, Bangkok Patana:** similar model — application, assessment,
shortlisted candidates may get **additional interviews or assessments** before offers.
Parents are typically interviewed too (family alignment with school philosophy).

## Science/scholarship boarding schools (KVIS, MWIT)

**KVIS (Kamnoetvidya Science Academy, Rayong — PTT-funded scholarship school) and
MWIT (Mahidol Wittayanusorn):** two-round selection.
- Round 1: written exams (math/science/English).
- Round 2: **portfolio submission + interview** for those passing Round 1.
- Interview probes scientific motivation, project experience, and readiness for a
  competitive boarding environment.

Source: https://admission.kvis.ac.th/en/faqs

## What the interviews actually test (cross-school synthesis)

Common assessment targets across MUIDS, IB schools, and scholarship programs:
1. **Communication & English fluency** — the dominant filter (TOEFL threshold at MUIDS;
   ELA/EAL level at NIST/UDIS).
2. **"Why this school / this program?"** — genuine, specific reasons (research the
   curriculum: IB learner profile, inquiry learning, CAS, DP subject choices).
3. **Self-knowledge** — strengths, weaknesses, hobbies, sports, instruments, free time.
4. **Academic motivation & future plans** — favourite subjects and why, university
   aspirations, how they handle challenge (STAR stories).
5. **Analytical/thinking skills** — scenario and "why do you think…" questions.
6. **Participation & personality** — engagement, enthusiasm, risk-taking (IB learner
   profile attributes: inquirer, thinker, communicator, principled, open-minded,
   caring, risk-taker, balanced, reflective).
7. **Parent/family alignment** (UDIS, NIST, most international schools) — support at
   home, community involvement, expectations of the school.

### High-yield practice question bank

Student-facing (from blog/practice sources, not school-confidential material):
- Tell me about yourself.
- Why do you want to join this school / the IB program?
- Which subjects do you like best and why?
- What do you do in your free time? Hobbies, sports, instruments?
- What are you good at? What do you want to improve?
- Tell me about a challenge you faced and how you handled it. (STAR)
- What kind of student are you? How do you work in a team?
- What do you know about our school? What interests you?
- Where do you see yourself in 5 years?
- Do you have any questions for us?

Parent-facing (students should know what parents may be asked):
- Why did you choose this school for your child?
- How do you support learning at home?
- How does your child respond to structure and discipline?
- What are your educational goals for your child?
- Are you prepared to participate in the school community?
